VA Loans in Washington
What Veteran Home Buyers Need to Know

Shi Hao Liu
Licensed WA Real Estate Broker ・ Kelly Right Real Estate ・ Updated November 15, 2026

A VA loan lets eligible veterans, active-duty service members, and some surviving spouses buy a home with no down payment and no monthly mortgage insurance — a meaningful advantage on the Eastside, where high prices make a 20% down payment hard to reach. Most veterans with full entitlement also face no VA-imposed loan limit in King County. Here's how eligibility, the funding fee, and the VA's property requirements actually work for a Washington purchase in 2026.

Who qualifies for a VA loan

VA loan eligibility is based on your service history, not your current job or income (your lender still evaluates income and credit separately). In general terms:

  • Active-duty service members and veterans typically qualify after meeting a minimum period of active duty, with the exact length depending on when and how you served.
  • National Guard and Reserve members can qualify through a minimum period of non-training active duty, or through enough creditable years in the Selected Reserve, even without an active-duty deployment.
  • Surviving spouses of service members who died in the line of duty or from a service-connected disability may also be eligible.
  • Your discharge generally needs to be under conditions other than dishonorable.

Because the exact service-time thresholds vary by era of service and situation, the only way to know for certain is to request your Certificate of Eligibility (COE) — the document that proves to a lender you qualify. Most lenders can pull your COE electronically in minutes; you can also request one yourself through VA.gov or by mailing VA Form 26-1880.

Zero down payment, and no monthly mortgage insurance

The headline benefit is that a VA loan can finance up to 100% of the purchase price — no down payment required. That's a real difference from an FHA loan (3.5% down) or a conventional loan (commonly 3-20% down), and it matters most in a market like the Eastside where even a modest down payment can mean six figures in cash. VA loans also skip the monthly private mortgage insurance that low-down-payment conventional and FHA loans charge, which can meaningfully lower your monthly payment even against a loan with a similar rate. You'll still need to budget for closing costs, prepaid property tax and insurance, and the funding fee below — a VA loan isn't a zero-cash purchase, just a zero-down one.

The VA funding fee: how much it costs

Most VA borrowers pay a one-time VA funding fee at closing, which helps keep the program funded for future veterans without relying on taxpayer subsidy. For a purchase or construction loan, the standard fee is based on your down payment and whether it's your first time using the benefit:

  • Less than 5% down: 2.15% of the loan amount on first use; 3.3% on subsequent use.
  • 5% up to 10% down: 1.5% of the loan amount, whether it's your first or a later use.
  • 10% or more down: 1.25% of the loan amount, whether it's your first or a later use.

On a $750,000 purchase with no down payment and first-time use, that's roughly $16,125 — a real cost, but one you can finance into the loan rather than pay in cash at closing. The fee schedule is set by the VA and has periodically changed in the past, so confirm the current percentage for your situation with your lender before you write an offer.

Who's exempt from the funding fee

You're generally exempt from the funding fee if you receive VA compensation for a service-connected disability, if you'd be entitled to that compensation but are instead receiving retirement or active-duty pay, or if you're a surviving spouse eligible for Dependency and Indemnity Compensation (DIC). Exemption isn't automatic just because you're a veteran — your lender checks it against your COE, so it's worth confirming early if you think you may qualify.

VA loan limits in King County: no cap for full entitlement

Since the Blue Water Navy Vietnam Veterans Act took effect on January 1, 2020, veterans and service members with full VA entitlement have had no VA-imposed loan limit at all — what you can actually borrow is governed by your lender's underwriting and the home's appraised value, not a county cap. That's a meaningful advantage in King County, where the 2026 conforming loan limit for other loan types is $1,063,750 — well below many Eastside home prices. If you have partial entitlement remaining, typically because you still have an existing VA loan, the county limit still applies to you. See our jumbo loan guide for how King County's conforming limit works for buyers using other loan types.

VA appraisals and Minimum Property Requirements

Every VA loan requires a VA-assigned appraiser to value the home and confirm it meets the VA's Minimum Property Requirements (MPRs) — baseline standards covering safety, structural soundness, and sanitation. On the kind of older housing stock common across Seattle and the Eastside, MPRs can flag things like a missing handrail on stairs, a failing roof, exposed wiring, or a non-functioning heating system, and typically those repairs need to be completed before the loan can close. This isn't unique to VA loans — many of the same issues would surface on a conventional appraisal or a standard home inspection — but a VA appraiser applies a specific checklist. Our guide to common inspection findings in Pacific Northwest homes covers several of the issues that tend to come up, and our home inspection contingency guide explains how to handle repair negotiations separately from the appraisal process.

Can a VA buyer compete in a multiple-offer Eastside market?

Some sellers and listing agents assume a VA offer is slower or riskier than a conventional one — that's mostly a myth. A VA loan closes on a similar timeline to a comparable conventional loan with an experienced lender, and VA buyers are not required to ask sellers to pay for specific repairs out of pocket in order for the loan to close (a seller can decline requested repairs, in which case the buyer can choose to cover them or the deal may fall through, just as with any other financing type). What actually helps a VA offer compete is the same as any offer: a strong, verified pre-approval letter, clean terms, and — when the market calls for it — tools like an escalation clause.
