Buying a Home on Mercer Island, WA
A 2026 Buyer's Guide

Shi Hao Liu
Licensed WA Real Estate Broker ・ Kelly Right Real Estate ・ Updated September 21, 2026

Mercer Island is a roughly 6.4-square-mile city sitting in the middle of Lake Washington between Seattle and Bellevue, connected to both by I-90. It's one of King County's most expensive markets — Redfin put the median sale price around $2.5 million as of July 2026 — but it's also the only Eastside-adjacent city with a single school district covering the whole island and, as of March 2026, its own light rail station. Here's what actually matters if you're considering a purchase here.

Where Mercer Island fits

Mercer Island became its own city on July 5, 1960, after residents voted to incorporate rather than be annexed into Seattle. Two parallel floating bridges cross the lake through it: the Lacey V. Murrow Memorial Bridge (opened 1940, connecting to Seattle's Mount Baker neighborhood) and the Homer M. Hadley Memorial Bridge (added in 1989), together carrying I-90 as an eight-lane freeway. That geography is the whole story of the island — it's a small, quiet, almost entirely residential community that happens to sit on the single busiest highway corridor between Seattle and the Eastside, including Bellevue, Redmond, and Kirkland.

The new light rail changes the commute math

Sound Transit's Mercer Island Station opened March 28, 2026, in the median of I-90 just north of Town Center, with an attached 447-stall park-and-ride. Riding the 2 Line from the island now takes about 10 minutes to downtown Bellevue, 10 minutes to Seattle's International District, and roughly 21 minutes to the University of Washington — trip times that don't move with bridge traffic. By car, drive times vary a lot by where on the island you live: from the north end, downtown Seattle and downtown Bellevue both typically run 10-20 minutes, while the south end of the island can run 20-45 minutes to either during peak traffic. For a buyer choosing between neighborhoods, that spread is worth weighing as carefully as square footage.

One school district, not three

Unlike Sammamish, where a single city is split across three districts, the entire island is served by one: Mercer Island School District 400, made up of four elementary schools, one middle school, and Mercer Island High School (with an alternative branch). That removes one common source of confusion for buyers — wherever you land on the island, your kids attend the same district — though it's still worth confirming your specific assigned elementary school, since attendance zones can shift within the island itself.

Mercer Island's neighborhoods

The island doesn't have Sammamish- or Issaquah-style named subdivisions so much as geographic zones radiating out from its commercial core:

  • Town Center — the island's commercial heart, with grocery stores, restaurants, the independent Island Books bookstore, and the largest concentration of condos and apartments on the island. This is where most non-single-family housing is.
  • North End / near I-90 — closest to both bridges and the new light rail station, generally the shortest commute to Seattle and Bellevue.
  • Mid-Island / Luther Burbank area — surrounds the island's largest park, with a mix of established single-family homes.
  • South End — larger lots and more waterfront frontage, generally the priciest and quietest part of the island, but the longest drive to either bridge.

What homes cost on Mercer Island in 2026

Mercer Island is consistently one of the most expensive cities in the Seattle area. As of July 2026, Redfin reported a median sale price around $2.5 million, down roughly 10.8% from a year earlier, while Zillow's typical home-value estimate for the island ran closer to $2.08 million over the same stretch. Both figures dwarf King County's overall median of about $838,000 in September 2026. Pricing varies significantly by neighborhood and waterfront access — South End listings have recently traded noticeably higher than homes in more central parts of the island — so a single city-wide median only tells you so much about any specific listing.

Luther Burbank Park and everyday life

The island's signature park, Luther Burbank Park, sits on the northeast shore: 77 acres with three-quarters of a mile of Lake Washington shoreline, a swimming beach, boat dock, fishing piers, tennis and pickleball courts, and several miles of trails. Between the park system and Town Center's walkable retail strip, day-to-day life on Mercer Island leans more compact and pedestrian-friendly than most Eastside suburbs — a meaningful trade-off against the larger lots you'd get in Sammamish or Issaquah for a comparable budget.

What to watch for as a buyer

  • Competitive listings still move fast. Recent Redfin data shows Mercer Island homes averaging around 2 offers and roughly 14 days on market — competitive by most standards, even with prices off their peak. Review our guide on how to win a bidding war on the Eastside before you write an offer.
  • Inspections on an older housing stock. Much of the island was built out well before the newer construction common in Sammamish or Issaquah. Our home inspection contingency guide covers when a sewer scope or structural inspection is worth adding, especially near the waterfront.
  • Condo and townhouse purchases in Town Center. If you're looking at one of the island's condos or attached homes rather than a single-family lot, read our HOA and condo documents guide before your review period runs out.
  • Bridge and construction noise near I-90. Homes closest to the freeway and the new transit station trade a shorter commute for more highway noise — worth an in-person visit at different times of day before you commit.
