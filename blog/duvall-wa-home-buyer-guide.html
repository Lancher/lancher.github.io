Buying a Home in Duvall, WA
A 2026 Buyer's Guide

Shi Hao Liu
Licensed WA Real Estate Broker ・ Kelly Right Real Estate ・ Updated November 27, 2026

Duvall is a small city of roughly 8,500 residents tucked into the Snoqualmie Valley on SR-203, about 8 miles from Redmond and Woodinville as the crow flies but with a noticeably more rural, small-town feel than either. Zillow's Home Value Index put the typical Duvall home at $930,832 as of August 2026 — close to King County's October 2026 single-family median of $925,000, which is unusual since most Eastside cities run well above the county number. Here's what to know about schools, flood risk, rural utilities, and the commute before you buy here.

Where Duvall fits on the map

Duvall sits on SR-203 in northeast King County, roughly halfway between Monroe to the north and Carnation to the south, in the Snoqualmie Valley. The town's current site was settled after the community relocated from nearby Cherry Valley, and Duvall incorporated on January 6, 1913. It has grown quickly in recent decades: U.S. Census figures put the population at 4,676 in 2000, 6,713 in 2010, and 8,035 in 2020, with a 2023 estimate around 8,517. Unlike Redmond or Bellevue, there's no direct freeway connection — getting to the Eastside job centers means Novelty Hill Road or the Woodinville-Duvall Road, both two-lane roads through farmland and forest rather than a limited-access highway.

Schools: the Riverview School District

Duvall and neighboring Carnation are both served by the Riverview School District, whose schools include Cedarcrest High School, Tolt Middle School, Cherry Valley Elementary, Stillwater Elementary, and Carnation Elementary. Niche gives the district an overall grade of B+ and an Academics grade of A, and Cherry Valley Elementary is generally cited as the district's top-performing elementary school in state test-score rankings. As with any district, individual school ratings shift year to year and attendance boundaries can change by street, so confirm the exact elementary, middle, and high school assignment for a specific address directly with the district before writing an offer.

A small-town core with a 31-mile trail out the back door

Duvall's compact historic downtown — relocated and rebuilt after the move from Cherry Valley — still has early-1900s storefronts, the restored Duvall Depot, and the Dougherty House along a heritage walking route maintained by the Snoqualmie Valley Museum. What sets it apart from other small Eastside-adjacent towns is what starts right behind that downtown: the Snoqualmie Valley Trail, a King County Parks multi-use trail built on a former rail line that runs roughly 31 miles from Duvall south to Rattlesnake Lake near North Bend, open to walking, cycling, and horseback riding. Combined with a meaningful number of larger rural lots on the valley floor and surrounding hillsides, Duvall draws a different kind of buyer than the denser Eastside cities — people looking for acreage, horse property, or simply more separation from neighbors, without giving up a walkable small-town center.

What homes cost in Duvall in 2026

Pricing data for a market this size varies more by source and month than it does for larger cities. Zillow's Home Value Index showed Duvall at $930,832 as of August 2026, down slightly year over year; Redfin's figures have ranged from roughly $930,000 to $955,000 across late 2025 and early 2026, depending on the month sampled. Either way, Duvall lands close to King County's October 2026 single-family median of $925,000 reported by Northwest MLS — a contrast with Redmond, Bellevue, or Kirkland, where typical prices run hundreds of thousands of dollars above the county figure. That makes Duvall worth a look for buyers who want more land or a lower price point than the core Eastside offers, provided the commute trade-off works for your routine. As always, treat citywide figures as a starting point — a half-acre property on the valley floor and an in-town lot a block from Main Street can price very differently, so ask your agent for comparables specific to the property type you're considering.

The commute reality

Duvall's distance to the Eastside job centers looks short on a map — roughly 8 miles to both Redmond and Woodinville — but there's no direct highway connection, so the drive runs along two-lane roads like Novelty Hill Road or the Woodinville-Duvall Road rather than an interstate. Census data puts the average commute time for Duvall workers at just over 30 minutes, broadly in line with other outlying King County communities, but peak-hour congestion on those two-lane routes can add significantly more on a bad day. If you're weighing a longer or less predictable commute against more land and a lower price, our Eastside relocation guide walks through how to think about that trade-off.

What to watch for as a buyer

  • Snoqualmie River flood risk. The Snoqualmie River runs through the valley near Duvall and Carnation, and King County's flood monitoring and past high-water events have shown the river can affect low-lying areas and, during serious flooding, even close some of the roads connecting the two towns. Check the FEMA flood zone designation for any specific address, and see our flood zone and flood insurance guide for how that process works and what coverage costs.
  • Septic and well systems on rural parcels. Outside the core of town, many properties — especially the larger, acreage-style lots that draw buyers here in the first place — rely on private septic systems and wells rather than municipal sewer and water. Confirm which utilities actually serve a specific property before you write an offer; our septic and well inspection guide covers what to check, including King County's property-transfer septic inspection requirement.
  • Older homes in the historic core. Homes in and around Duvall's original townsite can be considerably older than typical Eastside construction, which raises the usual questions about roofing, electrical, plumbing, and foundation condition. Our common PNW inspection findings guide covers what typically turns up.
  • Two-lane road access. Because Duvall isn't served by a freeway, both daily commuting and emergency access run through a small number of two-lane roads. Confirm how a specific property connects to SR-203 or the main arterials, particularly for homes further out on the valley floor or in the surrounding hills.
