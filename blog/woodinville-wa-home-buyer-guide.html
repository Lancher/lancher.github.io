Buying a Home in Woodinville, WA
A 2026 Buyer's Guide

Shi Hao Liu
Licensed WA Real Estate Broker ・ Kelly Right Real Estate ・ Updated September 22, 2026

Woodinville sits at the north end of the Eastside, where the Sammamish River Valley opens up into wineries, farmland, and large residential lots. Zillow put the typical Woodinville home value around $1.24 million in 2026, and Redfin's sale-price figures for the city have run higher still, generally in the $1.2 million to $1.4 million range. It's a single-school-district city best known for wine country — more than 100 wineries and tasting rooms packed into a few square miles — but for buyers it's really a story about lot size, a top-rated school district, and a commute to Redmond that's shorter than most people expect. Here's what actually matters if you're considering a purchase here.

Where Woodinville fits

Woodinville incorporated as its own city on March 27, 1993, after residents voted to break away rather than risk being annexed by neighboring Bothell — the third attempt at incorporation in eleven years, and the one that finally passed by 52 votes. The city covers roughly 5.7 square miles at the confluence of the Sammamish River and Bear Creek, and had an estimated population of about 14,350 as of April 2026. It sits north of Redmond, east of Bothell, and west of Duvall — close enough to the Eastside job centers to commute daily, but with a noticeably more rural, large-lot feel than Bellevue or Kirkland.

The commute is shorter than its "wine country" reputation suggests

Woodinville is about 7 miles from downtown Redmond via SR-202, a drive that typically runs 15-20 minutes outside of peak hours — closer than Sammamish or Issaquah. Bellevue and Kirkland are both roughly 20 minutes away via SR-522 and I-405, and downtown Seattle runs about 25-30 miles round-trip via SR-522, typically 30-45 minutes depending on traffic. For buyers relocating for a Microsoft, Amazon, or Google role in Redmond or Bellevue, Woodinville is worth cross-shopping against Sammamish for the shorter commute, and against Kirkland for the larger lots.

One school district covers nearly the whole city

Almost all of Woodinville falls within Northshore School District 417, which also serves Bothell and Kenmore and educates more than 21,000 students across over 30 schools — one of the higher-rated districts in the state. Woodinville itself is home to 11 Northshore schools, including seven elementary schools, two middle schools, and Woodinville High School. That's a simpler picture than Sammamish, where a single city is split across three districts — though it's still worth confirming your specific attendance zone, since Northshore's boundaries can shift within the city.

Woodinville's neighborhoods

Woodinville doesn't have a dense grid of named subdivisions so much as a handful of distinct pockets radiating out from its small downtown core:

  • Downtown Woodinville / Wedge — the walkable core near NE 175th St and 133rd Ave NE, closest to shops, restaurants, and the Warehouse District wineries.
  • Wellington (East & West) — one of the city's most sought-after residential areas, known for larger lots, often an acre or more, mature landscaping, and a quieter, more established feel.
  • Woodinville Heights — a hillside pocket above the Sammamish River Valley, close to downtown and city parks.
  • Hollywood District — a short drive northeast of downtown, centered on the historic Hollywood Schoolhouse and one of the two main wine-tasting clusters.
  • Reinwood / Leota — a residential area toward Bothell with a mix of home ages and sizes.

What homes cost in Woodinville in 2026

Woodinville is one of the pricier cities on the Eastside, driven largely by its inventory of larger single-family lots rather than dense new construction. Zillow's typical home value for the city ran around $1.24 million in mid-to-late 2026, down modestly from a year earlier. Redfin's median sale-price and list-price data for the same stretch has generally run higher, in the $1.2 million to $1.4 million range, though month-to-month figures from different data providers vary enough that any single number is only a rough guide — actual pricing depends heavily on lot size, view, and whether a property backs onto wine country or a more suburban block. Homes have recently gone under contract quickly, often within a couple of weeks of listing, so buyers should be prepared to move fast on well-priced properties.

Wine country, in your backyard

Woodinville is home to more than 100 wineries and tasting rooms — one of the largest concentrations of wine tourism in the state — split mainly between the Hollywood District and the Warehouse District near downtown. Chateau Ste. Michelle, Washington's oldest and largest winery, has anchored the area since 1976 and returned to local, Washington-family ownership in December 2025 after decades under out-of-state corporate owners. For buyers, that means weekend tourist traffic and event-night parking in the Hollywood District and around downtown are worth factoring in if you're looking at a home nearby, alongside the upside of walkable access to tasting rooms, restaurants, and the Sammamish River Trail.

What's changing downtown

The 19-acre site of the former Molbak's Garden + Home — a Woodinville fixture for 67 years before it closed in January 2024 — has been approved by the city for a large mixed-use redevelopment, planned to bring roughly 1,300 housing units along with retail, restaurants, and a hotel in phases over the coming years. Buyers looking at homes near downtown should expect construction activity and a gradually changing streetscape in that immediate area over the next several years.

What to watch for as a buyer

  • Competitive offers on well-priced listings. Woodinville's inventory has recently moved quickly, and desirable large-lot properties can still draw multiple offers. Our guide on how to win a bidding war on the Eastside covers escalation clauses and contingency strategy.
  • Septic systems and private wells on larger lots. Unlike most of the urban Eastside, some larger and more rural Woodinville parcels run on septic and well rather than city sewer and water — get a septic inspection and well test as part of your due diligence, in addition to a standard home inspection.
  • Older homes and aging infrastructure. Our home inspection contingency guide covers when a sewer scope or structural inspection is worth adding, particularly on homes built before the 1990s.
  • HOA rules in newer developments. Some of Woodinville's newer subdivisions carry an HOA; if you're looking at one, read our HOA and condo documents guide before your review period runs out.
