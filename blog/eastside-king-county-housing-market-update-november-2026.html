Eastside & King County
Housing Market Update: November 2026

Shi Hao Liu
Licensed WA Real Estate Broker ・ Kelly Right Real Estate ・ Updated November 23, 2026

The Eastside market has cooled from its 2024-2025 peak: Northwest MLS data put King County's October 2026 single-family median price down 7.2% year over year to $925,000, and inventory region-wide climbed to 4.41 months of supply from 3.02 a year earlier. Mortgage rates, meanwhile, moved the other way — Freddie Mac's survey had the 30-year fixed averaging 7.28% for the week of November 19, 2026. Translation: more homes to choose from, more negotiating room, and a higher monthly payment per dollar borrowed. Here's what the numbers actually say and what they mean if you're buying on the Eastside right now.

Where mortgage rates stand right now

Freddie Mac's Primary Mortgage Market Survey — the most widely cited weekly benchmark for U.S. mortgage rates — put the average 30-year fixed rate at 7.28% for the week of November 19, 2026, up from 7.11% the week before and well above the 6.26% average from roughly the same week a year earlier. The 15-year fixed averaged 6.66%, also up week over week. That's a national average, not a quote for any individual borrower; your actual rate depends on credit score, down payment, loan type, and lender, which is exactly why getting pre-approved with more than one lender is worth the extra effort before you start touring homes.

Rates matter more than the sticker price of a home for most buyers' monthly budget. A rate move of even half a percentage point changes the payment on a $900,000 loan by several hundred dollars a month — which is part of why buyer demand, and therefore pricing, has softened as rates climbed back above 7% this fall.

Across the broader NWMLS service area (26 counties in Western and Central Washington), closed sales fell to 5,332 in October 2026 — down 14.3% from 6,222 a year earlier — while the region-wide median price settled at $620,000, down 3.1% from $640,000. Months of inventory region-wide rose to 4.41, up from 3.02 a year earlier, meaning it would now take roughly four and a half months to sell through every home currently listed if no new listings were added — a meaningful loosening from the tight, under-3-month markets of the past few years.

The Eastside specifically: single-family vs. condo

County-wide figures understate how much higher Eastside prices run compared with King County overall, so it helps to look at Eastside-specific data. The most recent granular breakout available, from Realogics Sotheby's International Realty's Q2 2026 (April-June) market report, showed:

  • Eastside single-family homes: median sale price fell about 3.85%, from $1,560,000 to $1,500,000, year over year. Months of supply rose from 2.5 to 3.5, and average days on market roughly doubled, from about 16 to about 29 days.
  • Eastside condos: median sale price fell from $724,000 to $637,000, and months of supply reached 5.5 — a shift from a seller's market into what RSIR classifies as a neutral, balanced market.

Those numbers are now several months old, but the direction lines up with King County's more recent October figures — inventory building, days on market lengthening, and prices softening from last year's peak — which suggests the Eastside's own numbers have likely moved further in the same direction since Q2, not reversed.

What "months of supply" means for your offer strategy

Months of supply measures how long it would take to sell every home currently on the market at the current sales pace — it's the standard way agents and analysts gauge who holds the leverage. A common rule of thumb: under about 4 months favors sellers, 4 to 6 months is roughly balanced, and above 6 months favors buyers. By that measure, King County and the Eastside single-family market sit right around the line between a seller's market and a balanced one — tighter than a true buyer's market, but with noticeably more room than a year ago. Eastside condos, with 5.5 months of supply as of Q2 2026, have moved further toward buyer-favorable territory than single-family homes have.

What this means if you're buying a single-family home

You likely still won't get a steep discount on a well-priced home in a top school district — those still move quickly and can draw multiple offers, especially under $1.5 million. But you have more room than buyers had in 2024-2025 to ask for an inspection response, negotiate a closing-cost or rate-buydown credit, or walk away from a home that doesn't check your boxes without feeling like you'll never see another one. If you do find yourself in competition, our guides to escalation clauses and winning a bidding war cover how to compete without overpaying.

What this means if you're buying a condo

With Eastside condo supply running higher than single-family supply, condo buyers generally have more negotiating leverage right now — more time to think, more room to ask for repairs or credits, and less pressure to waive contingencies. That's worth factoring in if you're weighing a condo against a townhouse: a softer condo market can make the math look different than it did a year or two ago, though HOA dues, reserves, and building-specific financing rules still deserve the same scrutiny regardless of market conditions.

Using softer conditions to negotiate, not just to wait

A cooler market doesn't just mean lower prices — it often means sellers are more open to covering costs that used to be entirely on the buyer. A seller-paid rate buydown or closing-cost credit can offset today's higher rates more effectively than holding out for a lower list price, and it's increasingly realistic to ask for in a market with 4+ months of supply. Likewise, a full home inspection contingency is more often back on the table as a normal part of an offer rather than something buyers feel pressured to waive to compete.
