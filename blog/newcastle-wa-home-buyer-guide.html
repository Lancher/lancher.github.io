Buying a Home in Newcastle, WA
A 2026 Buyer's Guide

Shi Hao Liu
Licensed WA Real Estate Broker ・ Kelly Right Real Estate ・ Updated October 13, 2026

Newcastle is a small, 4.5-square-mile city of roughly 13,900 people tucked between Bellevue's Factoria area, Renton, and Issaquah along I-405. Unlike most Eastside cities, it has no light rail and no single school district of its own — it's split between Bellevue, Issaquah, and Renton school districts depending on the address. What it does have is a 350-acre golf club with two public championship courses, a coal-mining history going back to the 1860s, and a median sale price around $1.54 million as of October 2026. Here's what actually matters if you're considering a purchase here.

Where Newcastle fits

Newcastle sits on the west slope of Cougar Mountain, wedged between Bellevue to the north, Renton to the south, and Issaquah to the east. It's reached off I-405 at Exit 9 (112th Avenue SE) or Exit 10, which also serves Bellevue's Factoria retail and office district via Coal Creek Parkway — the main road that runs southeast from the freeway into the city center. There's no Sound Transit light rail station in Newcastle itself, so getting around means driving; the city's own real estate marketing puts the drive to downtown Bellevue at roughly 10-15 minutes and to Renton at around 10 minutes, though both vary with traffic on I-405.

A name with a coal-mining past

The city didn't start out as "Newcastle." According to the City of Newcastle's own history, residents voted in April 1993 to incorporate the area, and the city officially formed on September 30, 1994 under the name Newport Hills. A follow-up vote that November changed the name to Newcastle, reviving the name of a 19th-century coal-mining settlement that once stood near what's now Lake Boren. Coal was discovered on Cougar Mountain in the 1860s, and by the city's own account, nearly 11 million tons were extracted from the area's mines between 1869 and 1963 — making Newcastle one of the most productive coal-mining districts in Washington's early history before the mines closed and the area returned to forest and residential development.

Three school districts, not one

This is the detail that trips up the most buyers. Where Mercer Island is served by a single district covering the whole city, Newcastle's own website lists three separate public school districts serving different parts of the city: Bellevue School District, Issaquah School District, and Renton School District. A commercial real estate guide generalizes that the northern part of the city tends to fall in Bellevue School District and the southern part in Issaquah School District, with Renton School District covering other sections — but district lines don't track neatly with neighborhood names or even individual streets. Before you fall for a specific house, verify the assigned elementary, middle, and high school directly through each district's address-lookup tool, the same way you would in Sammamish, where boundaries split a single city three ways as well.

The Golf Club at Newcastle

Newcastle's best-known landmark is golf, not geography. The Golf Club at Newcastle sits on a 350-acre site on Cougar Mountain's lower slopes and includes two public, 18-hole championship courses — Coal Creek and China Creek — along with clubhouse dining and views toward Lake Washington and the Seattle skyline on a clear day. The club opened in 1999 and anchors the upscale homes built around its fairways, which tend to carry a premium over the rest of the city. If you're looking at a home bordering the course, ask about any golf-course-related HOA or easement terms in addition to the standard resale documents.

Lake Boren and everyday life

Lake Boren Park, near the site of the original 1800s coal town, is Newcastle's community hub — the city hosts events like Concerts in the Park and the annual Newcastle Days celebration there. Outside the golf club and the park, the city is mostly residential: single-family neighborhoods along and above Coal Creek Parkway, with May Creek's park and trail system running through the city's eastern side toward Issaquah. Newcastle doesn't have the dozens of named master-planned subdivisions you'd find in Sammamish or Issaquah; it reads more as a handful of residential pockets organized around the golf club, the lake, and the I-405 corridor.

What homes cost in Newcastle in 2026

As of October 2026, Redfin puts Newcastle's median sale price at roughly $1.54 million, with single-family homes slightly higher at about $1.545 million, townhouses around $1.09 million, and condos considerably lower at roughly $405,000 — reflecting a small condo stock concentrated in a few developments rather than a broad market. Northwest MLS's September 2026 snapshot put King County's overall median (homes and condos combined) at $850,000, which puts Newcastle well above the countywide figure — consistent with a small city built mostly of larger, newer single-family homes near a premium golf amenity. As with any city-level median, pricing varies a lot by proximity to the golf club, lot size, and school district assignment, so treat these figures as a starting point rather than a prediction for any specific home.

What to watch for as a buyer

  • Confirm your school district before you fall in love with a house. With three districts serving one small city, two homes a few blocks apart can feed completely different elementary, middle, and high schools.
  • Factor in the commute without light rail. Newcastle works well if you're driving to Bellevue, Renton, or Issaquah; it's a tougher fit if you're counting on transit for a Seattle commute.
  • Ask about golf-course-adjacent terms. Homes near the Golf Club at Newcastle can carry HOA rules, easements, or assessments tied to the course — read the resale certificate closely. See our HOA and condo documents guide before your review period runs out.
  • Budget for an inspection on the area's hillside lots. Cougar Mountain's slopes mean drainage and grading matter as much here as anywhere on the Eastside. Our home inspection contingency guide covers when extra specialist inspections are worth adding.
