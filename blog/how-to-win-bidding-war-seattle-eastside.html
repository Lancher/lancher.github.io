How to Win a Bidding War
in Seattle & the Eastside

Shi Hao Liu
Licensed WA Real Estate Broker ・ Kelly Right Real Estate ・ Updated July 4, 2026

In Seattle and the Eastside, well-priced homes routinely draw multiple offers within days. Winning rarely means simply bidding the most — it means structuring a clean, competitive offer: the right price strategy, smart contingency decisions, strong earnest money, and terms that make a seller pick you. Here's how to compete and win without overpaying.

Get fully pre-approved first

Before you write a single offer, have a full underwritten pre-approval — not just a pre-qualification. In a fast market you often can't tour seriously without one, and a strong financing letter tells the seller your deal will actually close. Line up a responsive local lender who can update your letter to match each offer amount quickly.

Understand the local competition

Offer strategy depends on the specific listing and neighborhood. A well-priced home in Redmond, Bellevue, or Kirkland may see several offers and sell above list, while an overpriced or dated home may sit. Recent comparable sales — available through the Northwest MLS — tell you what it will realistically take to win.

Use an escalation clause (carefully)

An escalation clause automatically raises your offer above competing bids by a set increment — say $10,000 — up to a maximum you choose. It can win a home without you guessing too high. The trade-offs: it reveals your ceiling, and it only triggers with a genuine competing offer (sellers must typically provide proof). Use it when competition is likely and you have a firm walk-away number.

Make your contingencies competitive

Contingencies protect you — but too many can sink your offer against cleaner bids. Common levers in Washington:

  • Pre-inspection: inspect before offering so you can shorten or waive the inspection contingency with eyes open.
  • Financing & appraisal: a larger down payment or an appraisal gap guarantee reassures sellers your deal survives a low appraisal.
  • Timelines: tighter (but realistic) contingency and closing timelines signal a smooth, fast close.

Never waive a protection you don't understand — winning a home you can't afford to close on is no win.

Strengthen earnest money and terms

Earnest money in the region typically runs 1–3% of the price. A larger deposit — and offering flexible possession dates that fit the seller's move — can tip a close decision your way. Small, thoughtful terms often matter as much as a few thousand dollars in price.

Write clean and respond fast

An error-free, complete offer using current NWMLS forms, submitted promptly with your pre-approval and proof of funds, tells the listing agent you're serious and low-risk. In a bidding war, speed and precision win deals that a slow, sloppy offer loses.

Where a flat fee agent fits in

Winning a bidding war is about strategy and execution, not commission. A flat $2,999 buyer's agent writes and negotiates a sharp, competitive offer and manages every deadline to closing — while you keep tens of thousands versus a 2.5% commission.
