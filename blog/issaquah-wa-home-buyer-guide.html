Buying a Home in Issaquah, WA
A 2026 Buyer's Guide

Shi Hao Liu
Licensed WA Real Estate Broker ・ Kelly Right Real Estate ・ Updated September 20, 2026

Issaquah is an Eastside city of roughly 40,000 residents at the foot of the Issaquah Alps, wedged between Lake Sammamish and I-90 about 17 miles east of Seattle. It's home to Costco's world headquarters, a genuinely walkable historic downtown, and one of the region's most-attended fall festivals - and it draws buyers who want Eastside schools and freeway access without giving up a town center. Prices vary widely by neighborhood, but citywide median sale prices ran roughly $1.0-1.1 million through mid-2026. This guide walks through schools, neighborhoods, commute, and what to watch for before you write an offer.

Where Issaquah fits on the Eastside

Issaquah sits at the junction of I-90 and I-405, directly on the freeway in a way that Sammamish - its neighbor to the north - is not. That freeway access is one of the biggest practical differences between the two cities: Issaquah commuters get straight onto I-90 toward Bellevue and Seattle, while Sammamish relies on surface arterials like SR-202 to reach the same freeways. Issaquah is bordered by Sammamish to the north, Renton and Newcastle to the west, and unincorporated King County - including Squak Mountain, Tiger Mountain, and the town of Preston - to the south and east, which is part of why the city functions as something of a gateway between the Eastside suburbs and the Cascade foothills.

Schools: mostly one district, but confirm the address

Almost all of the city of Issaquah falls within the Issaquah School District (No. 411), which also extends well beyond city limits into parts of Sammamish, Newcastle, Bellevue, Renton, Hobart, and Mirrormont. That's simpler than Sammamish, which splits across three separate districts - but district lines still don't always follow street-by-street intuition, and nearby unincorporated pockets like East Renton Highlands can fall into Renton or Tahoma school districts instead. Before writing an offer, confirm the assigned elementary, middle, and high school for the specific address using the district's own school-finder tool, since assignment can affect both your daily routine and resale value.

Issaquah's neighborhoods

Issaquah has more architectural and price variety than many Eastside cities, spanning a 19th-century mining-and-farming town core, master-planned hillside communities, and newer urban-style development:

  • Olde Town Issaquah — the historic downtown core, with independent shops, restaurants, breweries, and galleries in a genuinely walkable setting; homes here tend to be older and smaller than in the newer master-planned neighborhoods.
  • Issaquah Highlands — a large planned community on Grand Ridge northeast of downtown, built out since the late 1990s with a mix of townhomes, condos, and single-family homes, its own town center, and an HOA; this is generally the priciest and newest part of the city.
  • Talus — a 630-acre master-planned community on Cougar Mountain, governed by the Talus Residential Association, with roughly three-quarters of the land permanently preserved as open space bordering Cougar Mountain and Squak Mountain regional parks.
  • Squak Mountain — a wooded residential area between downtown and Squak Mountain State Park, with a mix of home styles from the 1960s through the early 2000s; some pockets have an HOA, others don't.
  • Gilman / North Issaquah — home to Gilman Village's shops and restaurants set in restored 1970s-era farm buildings, plus a mix of established and newer housing near I-90.

What homes cost in Issaquah in 2026

Issaquah's citywide numbers move around more than most Eastside cities because they blend very different pockets of the market. Redfin's citywide median sale price figures for Issaquah ran roughly $1.0 million to $1.1 million across the summer of 2026, while neighborhood-level data showed the spread clearly: Issaquah Highlands recently posted a median sale price near $1.3 million, while North Issaquah's smaller sample of sales ran well under $1 million over the same window. For comparison, King County's overall median sale price was about $890,000 in June 2026. Because a handful of luxury or starter-home sales can swing a monthly citywide median significantly, ask your agent for neighborhood-specific comparables rather than relying on a single citywide figure.

Costco's hometown, and a real downtown

Issaquah has been home to Costco Wholesale's world headquarters since 1994, with a corporate campus in central Issaquah that expanded further with a 2023 addition - making the company one of the city's largest employers and a steady source of local jobs and tax base. Unlike Sammamish, which is still years into building out its planned town center, Issaquah already has a functioning historic downtown: Olde Town's shops and restaurants, the Saturday Farmers Market at Pickering Barn (May through September), and the annual Salmon Days festival every October, which draws well over 100,000 visitors to watch salmon return to the Issaquah Creek fish ladder. For buyers who want walkable errands and a town center feel alongside Eastside schools, that's a meaningful differentiator.

Commute reality

Because Issaquah sits directly on I-90, the commute to Bellevue and Seattle tends to be faster and more predictable than from cities that rely on surface arterials to reach a freeway. The roughly nine-mile trip to Bellevue typically runs in the ballpark of 15-45 minutes depending on time of day and destination within Bellevue, with Seattle usually another 15-20 minutes beyond that during peak commute hours. Issaquah also has park-and-ride lots with bus service into Bellevue and Seattle, giving commuters a transit option that Sammamish mostly lacks. What to watch for as a buyer

  • HOA documents in the master-planned communities. Issaquah Highlands and Talus both carry HOA dues and governing documents you'll want to review carefully before your review period ends. See our guide to HOA and condo documents in Washington for what the resale certificate covers and your rights after you receive it.
  • Competitive offers on well-priced listings. With inventory running lean in parts of the city, a well-priced Issaquah home can still draw multiple offers. Our guide on how to win a bidding war on the Eastside covers escalation clauses and contingency strategy.
  • Older homes near downtown and on Squak Mountain. Homes from the 1960s through 1990s can hide roof, drainage, or aging-sewer issues a walkthrough won't reveal. Our home inspection contingency guide covers when a sewer scope or structural inspection is worth the cost, and when waiving inspection is too risky.
  • Wildfire and slope considerations near the Issaquah Alps. Homes backing onto Cougar, Squak, or Tiger Mountain sit closer to forested slopes than most Eastside listings; ask about defensible space, drainage, and any geotech reports on file for hillside lots.
