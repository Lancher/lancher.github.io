Eastside School Districts
& Home Prices: A 2026 Buyer's Guide

Shi Hao Liu
Licensed WA Real Estate Broker ・ Kelly Right Real Estate ・ Updated October 17, 2026

On the Eastside, which school district a home sits in is a street-by-street fact, not a city-by-city one. Redmond and Kirkland fall almost entirely in Lake Washington School District, but Sammamish, Newcastle, and Bellevue are each split between two or three separate districts — and the difference can mean a different elementary, middle, and high school for homes a few blocks apart. District also tracks loosely with price: Mercer Island and Bellevue carry the Eastside's highest medians, while Renton stays the most affordable city near the job corridor. Here's how the districts map to each city, where the boundaries get messy, and how 2026 prices compare.

Why this matters for your offer, not just your kids

Even buyers without school-age children should care about school district assignment: it's one of the biggest drivers of resale value and buyer demand on the Eastside, and it shows up in how many offers a listing draws. A home in a highly sought-after elementary boundary can get more competition — and a higher closing price — than a nearly identical home a few blocks away in a different attendance area. Confirming the assigned schools for a specific address, not just the city, should happen before you fall for a listing, not after you've written an offer.

The Eastside's main school districts, city by city

  • Lake Washington School District (LWSD) — the primary district for Redmond and Kirkland, and it also serves roughly the northern half of Sammamish, plus parts of Woodinville, Bellevue, and unincorporated King County. LWSD enrolls more than 30,000 students across a roughly 76-square-mile footprint, making it one of the larger districts in the state.
  • Bellevue School District — serves the city of Bellevue itself, running 28 schools district-wide, including Spanish- and Mandarin-immersion elementary programs and several choice schools. A portion of Bellevue's eastern and southern edges fall outside the district, in Issaquah or Renton School District instead.
  • Issaquah School District (No. 411) — serves the city of Issaquah along with the southern part of Sammamish, Newcastle, and portions of Bellevue, Renton, Hobart, and Mirrormont. It enrolled roughly 19,000–19,300 students across 29 schools in the 2024-25 school year, one of the larger districts on the Eastside.
  • Northshore School District (No. 417) — serves Bothell, Woodinville, and Kenmore, plus parts of unincorporated King and Snohomish counties. The district runs more than 30 schools for over 21,000 students and is highly rated statewide.
  • Renton School District (No. 403) — serves Renton along with parts of Newcastle, Bellevue, Kent, Tukwila, and unincorporated King County. It enrolled roughly 13,400 students in fall 2025.
  • Mercer Island School District — a small, self-contained district serving only Mercer Island, with about six schools and roughly 4,000 students. Because it covers one city and nothing else, it's the one Eastside district where you don't need to check a boundary map.
  • Snoqualmie Valley School District — covers a small pocket in northeast Sammamish, an area that has spent years petitioning (so far unsuccessfully) to be redrawn into Lake Washington School District, including an ongoing court appeal after the district's board again declined to move the boundary in late 2025.

Where one city splits between districts

A city name is a starting point, not an answer. Three Eastside cities are worth double-checking address by address:

  • Sammamish splits mainly between Lake Washington School District (north) and Issaquah School District (south), with that small Snoqualmie Valley pocket in the northeast.
  • Newcastle splits across Bellevue, Issaquah, and Renton School Districts, with no single district covering the whole city.
  • Bellevue is mostly served by its namesake district, but eastern and southern slivers fall into Issaquah or Renton School District instead.

In every case, use the specific district's own boundary or school-finder tool and the exact address — not the city name, ZIP code, or a neighborhood label — before you assume which schools a home is assigned to.

Boundaries can change after you buy

School attendance boundaries aren't fixed forever. Lake Washington School District has redrawn Redmond-area elementary and middle school boundaries as new schools opened, and separately adjusted Kirkland-area middle school boundaries (effective fall 2024) to manage overcrowding at Kirkland Middle School and Rose Hill Middle School. If a specific school matters enough to factor into your offer, it's worth checking whether the district has an open or recently closed boundary review for that attendance area.

How district tracks with 2026 home prices

District and price correlate, but loosely — lot size, home age, and commute all move the number too. Based on each city's own median for 2026:

  • Mercer Island (Mercer Island SD): Redfin put the median sale price at roughly $2.5 million as of July 2026 — the Eastside's highest, reflecting the island's limited supply and single top-rated district.
  • Bellevue (Bellevue SD, with eastern/southern edges in Issaquah or Renton SD): median around $1.5 million in 2026.
  • Sammamish (split LWSD / Issaquah SD): Redfin and Zillow both put the median around $1.6 million in spring 2026, regardless of which half of the city.
  • Kirkland (LWSD): median around $1.4 million in 2026.
  • Redmond (LWSD): median around $1.3 million in 2026.
  • Woodinville (Northshore SD): Zillow's typical home value ran around $1.24 million in mid-to-late 2026.
  • Bothell (Northshore SD): Zillow put the typical value just over $1.01 million as of July 2026.
  • Kenmore (Northshore SD): closed-sale median ran close to $990,000 over the three months ending July 2026.
  • Issaquah (Issaquah SD): Redfin's citywide figures ran roughly $1.0–$1.1 million over summer 2026.
  • Renton (Renton SD): Redfin put the citywide median at roughly $670,000 in June 2026 — the most affordable city near the Eastside job corridor by a wide margin.

For comparison, Northwest MLS reported a King County median sale price (homes and condos combined) of roughly $840,000 in September 2026. Every city above sits above that county median except Renton — and Renton's own public schools serve a district that still enrolls well over 10,000 students and anchors one of the Eastside's largest employers, Boeing's 737 plant. These figures are citywide averages; ask your agent for comparables inside the specific attendance boundary you're considering, since a top-rated elementary's boundary can carry a real premium over a less sought-after one a short drive away.

A sensible way to search

  1. Pick your priority districts first, not your priority cities — since several cities span more than one.
  2. Use each district's own boundary lookup tool for any specific address you're seriously considering, rather than trusting a listing site's auto-filled "schools" tab, which can be outdated or imprecise near a boundary line.
  3. Ask about pending boundary reviews for that attendance area, especially in fast-growing parts of Redmond, Kirkland, or Sammamish.
  4. Weigh district against commute and home type.
