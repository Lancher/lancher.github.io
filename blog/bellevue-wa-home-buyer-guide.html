Buying a Home in Bellevue, WA
A 2026 Buyer's Guide

Shi Hao Liu
Licensed WA Real Estate Broker ・ Kelly Right Real Estate ・ Updated October 27, 2026

Bellevue is the Eastside's largest and most urban city — Amazon's biggest Eastside hub, T-Mobile's U.S. headquarters, and now a full light rail connection across Lake Washington to Seattle. Zillow put Bellevue's typical home value at about $1.47 million as of mid-2026, down roughly 4% from a year earlier, with a median sale price closer to $1.38 million. Prices swing hard by neighborhood, from downtown condos to multimillion-dollar homes on the west side near the lake. Here's what actually matters if you're considering a purchase here.

Where Bellevue fits

Bellevue sits on the central Eastside, bordered by Lake Washington and Mercer Island to the west, Kirkland to the north, Redmond and Sammamish to the east, and Newcastle and Issaquah to the south. It's roughly 10 miles from downtown Seattle via I-90 or SR-520. With an estimated population around 155,000-158,000 as of 2025, Bellevue is the fifth-largest city in Washington and the largest on the Eastside — noticeably bigger than Redmond or Sammamish. The city spans several ZIP codes, including 98004 (Downtown and West Bellevue), 98005 (Northeast Bellevue), 98006 (Somerset, Newport, West Lake Sammamish), 98007 (Crossroads, BelRed), and 98008 (Lake Hills, Robinswood) — each with a different price tier, so always check which ZIP a specific listing falls in before comparing it to another.

Getting around: I-90, SR-520, and light rail across the lake

Bellevue sits at the junction of two major highways — I-90 to the south and SR-520 to the north — both crossing Lake Washington into Seattle, with SR-520 operating as a tolled bridge. The biggest recent change for buyers is transit: Sound Transit's 2 Line began running the full stretch across Lake Washington on March 28, 2026, when the Crosslake Connection opened, completing a one-seat light rail ride between Seattle, Bellevue, and Redmond (the Bellevue-to-Redmond segment had already been running since April 2024). Bellevue has six of its own stations: South Bellevue (serving Eastgate, Factoria, and Coal Creek commuters with a large park-and-ride), East Main, Bellevue Downtown (near City Hall and Meydenbauer Center), Wilburton, and two stations in the BelRed corridor at 120th Ave NE and 130th Ave NE. For buyers who commute into Seattle, proximity to one of these stations has become a meaningful factor in home value and livability.

Mostly one school district

Most of Bellevue is served by Bellevue School District (No. 405), which also covers the small enclave cities of Clyde Hill, Medina, Hunts Point, Yarrow Point, and Beaux Arts Village, plus portions of neighboring Newcastle, Kirkland, Issaquah, Redmond, and unincorporated King County. That makes Bellevue simpler than nearby cities like Sammamish or Redmond, where a single city can split across two or three districts — but don't assume every Bellevue-adjacent address is actually zoned to Bellevue schools. Confirm the exact attendance area for a specific address with the district before you buy. For how school districts compare across the broader Eastside, see our Eastside school districts guide.

Bellevue's neighborhoods

Bellevue's 16 officially recognized neighborhood areas range from dense urban cores to quiet, large-lot residential pockets:

  • Downtown — the city's walkable, high-rise urban center, with Bellevue Square, Lincoln Square, and The Bravern anchoring retail and office towers, plus its own light rail station.
  • BelRed and Wilburton — formerly industrial corridors now rezoned for dense mixed-use development, with two new light rail stations running through BelRed.
  • Crossroads — known for large apartment complexes and the Crossroads Shopping Center, one of the most ethnically diverse parts of the city.
  • Somerset — a hillside neighborhood of mostly spacious single-family homes with lake and mountain views, among Bellevue's priciest areas.
  • Newport — includes Newport Hills, with a walkable retail core, and Newport Shores, a Coal Creek waterfront pocket with private moorage.
  • Eastgate/Factoria — a retail center near I-90 surrounded by family residential streets, served by the South Bellevue light rail station.
  • West Bellevue — lakefront and near-lakefront areas bordering Medina and Clyde Hill, among the most expensive residential areas on the Eastside.

What homes cost in Bellevue in 2026

Zillow's typical home value for Bellevue ran about $1.47 million as of June 30, 2026, down roughly 3.9% from a year earlier, with a median sale price closer to $1.38 million. That's well above King County's countywide median sale price of $845,000 in August 2026 per NWMLS data. Price varies enormously by neighborhood and home type — Zillow's own neighborhood data put Northwest Bellevue above $2.3 million while Downtown condos and townhomes ran closer to $1.1 million over the same period. As with any citywide figure, ask your agent for comparables specific to the exact neighborhood and property type you're considering rather than relying on a single city-level number.

Amazon, T-Mobile, and a growing downtown

Bellevue's job base is one of the largest and fastest-growing on the Eastside. Amazon is the city's largest employer, with roughly 14,300 employees there as of the city's 2025 figures, and has publicly announced plans to grow its downtown Bellevue campus toward about 25,000 jobs. T-Mobile's U.S. headquarters is based in Bellevue, with roughly 7,800 employees per the same city data. Microsoft, though headquartered in neighboring Redmond, also employs more than 6,000 people at its downtown Bellevue offices, and the city is home to the headquarters of Paccar, Eddie Bauer, Valve, Bungie, Symetra Financial, and Puget Sound Energy, among others. That concentration of high-paying jobs is a major reason competition for well-priced Bellevue listings stays strong even when rates are high.

What to watch for as a buyer

  • Competitive offers on well-priced listings. Bellevue's deep job base keeps demand strong near downtown, light rail stations, and top school boundaries. Our guides on winning a bidding war and escalation clauses cover strategy and terms.
  • Which ZIP and attendance zone actually apply. Price tiers and school assignment can shift sharply within a few blocks, especially near the city's borders with Newcastle, Kirkland, Issaquah, or Redmond — confirm both before comparing listings.
  • Older homes in established neighborhoods. Homes from the 1960s-1980s in areas like Lake Hills or Newport can hide aging wiring, plumbing, or drainage issues a walkthrough won't reveal. See our home inspection contingency guide and common PNW inspection findings.
  • Ongoing construction near light rail and BelRed. Transit-oriented redevelopment around the Wilburton and BelRed stations means periodic construction noise and traffic in those immediate areas for years to come.
