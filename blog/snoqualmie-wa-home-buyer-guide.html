Buying a Home in Snoqualmie, WA
A 2026 Buyer's Guide

Shi Hao Liu
Licensed WA Real Estate Broker ・ Kelly Right Real Estate ・ Updated November 5, 2026

Snoqualmie is a city of roughly 13,500-14,100 residents at the far eastern end of King County's I-90 corridor, built around a historic railroad town and the master-planned golf community of Snoqualmie Ridge. Zillow put the typical home value here at $1,061,472 as of August 2026 — above the King County average despite a longer commute than Issaquah or Bellevue. It's a city that trades drive time for space, newer construction, and some of the highest-rated schools in the state. Here's what to know before you buy.

Where Snoqualmie fits on the map

Snoqualmie sits just northeast of North Bend, about 28 miles from downtown Seattle by road, at the point where the I-90 corridor starts climbing toward Snoqualmie Pass and the Cascades. That puts it noticeably farther from Seattle, Bellevue, and Redmond than core Eastside cities like Issaquah or Sammamish — but it's also why homes here come with more land, newer construction, and a small-town feel that's increasingly hard to find closer to Seattle. The Snoqualmie River runs along and through parts of the city and valley, which shapes both the scenery and, as covered below, some real flood considerations for buyers.

Schools: a top-tier district

Snoqualmie is served by the Snoqualmie Valley School District (SVSD 410), which also covers North Bend and Fall City. Third-party ranking sites such as SchoolDigger consistently place SVSD among the top tier of Washington's roughly 295 school districts, with Mount Si High School as the comprehensive high school for most of the city and Two Rivers School serving as a smaller alternative high school. As with any district, boundaries can shift by street and specific school ratings change year to year, so confirm the assigned elementary, middle, and high school for a given address directly with the district before writing an offer.

Snoqualmie Ridge: the master-planned community

Most of the newer housing stock in Snoqualmie sits in Snoqualmie Ridge, a roughly 1,300-acre master-planned community the city annexed in 1990, with construction beginning in 1998 under original developer Quadrant Homes. The community is built around The Club at Snoqualmie Ridge, a Jack Nicklaus-designed golf course that opened in 1999 and once served as a PGA Tour Champions venue, hosting the Boeing Classic. Snoqualmie Ridge has its own town center and business park with shopping, medical clinics, trails, and parks, all governed by a residential owners' association with its own dues and CC&Rs. New infill construction continued into 2026, with builder Tri Pointe Homes marketing townhome-style product as among the last new-construction opportunities left in the community — a sign the Ridge is close to fully built out.

Historic downtown and the rest of the city

Outside Snoqualmie Ridge, the older part of the city centers on a walkable historic downtown tied to the area's railroad and logging history. The Northwest Railway Museum runs heritage train rides from the depot on weekends between April and October, and the Snoqualmie Valley Trail — a converted rail corridor running roughly 31 miles from Duvall through Snoqualmie and North Bend toward Rattlesnake Lake — gives residents a long, mostly flat trail for walking and cycling. Just outside downtown, Snoqualmie Falls, a roughly 270-foot waterfall, is one of the most-visited natural landmarks in the state. Homes near the historic core tend to be older and smaller than Ridge construction, with a mix of lot sizes and architectural styles typical of a town that predates its master-planned neighbor by a century.

What homes cost in Snoqualmie in 2026

Zillow's home value index put the typical Snoqualmie home at $1,061,472 as of August 31, 2026, down about 2.7% from a year earlier and roughly flat month-over-month — compared to a King County-wide typical value of $835,385 over the same period. That's a meaningful premium over the county average for a city this far from Seattle, driven largely by the newer, larger homes in Snoqualmie Ridge. As with any citywide figure, it blends very different housing stock — compact older homes near downtown and larger newer construction on the Ridge — so ask your agent for comparables specific to the neighborhood and home type you're considering rather than relying on a single citywide number.

The commute reality

Snoqualmie's location is the main trade-off against its schools and newer housing stock. The city sits roughly 28 miles from downtown Seattle via I-90, noticeably farther than closer-in Eastside cities, and I-90 traffic between North Bend and Issaquah can add significant time during peak commute hours, particularly around weather or winter travel toward Snoqualmie Pass. Buyers choosing Snoqualmie are generally trading a longer, less predictable commute for more space and newer construction at a given price point than they'd find closer to Seattle, Bellevue, or Redmond. What to watch for as a buyer

  • Flood risk near the river and low ground. The Snoqualmie River and surrounding valley have a history of seasonal flooding, and some low-lying parcels in and around the city sit in or near a mapped flood zone. Check the FEMA flood zone designation for any specific address and ask about flood insurance costs before you fall in love with a listing near the river — see our flood zone and flood insurance guide for how that process works.
  • HOA dues and governing documents in Snoqualmie Ridge. Homes in the Ridge carry HOA dues and CC&Rs tied to the golf course, trails, and shared amenities. Review the resale certificate carefully during your review period — our HOA and condo documents guide covers what to look for.
  • Older homes near downtown. Homes outside the Ridge can be considerably older, which raises the usual questions about roofing, plumbing, electrical, and — depending on the lot — septic or well service. Our septic and well inspection guide and common PNW inspection findings guide cover what typically turns up.
  • New-construction contracts in the Ridge's remaining infill. If you're buying new construction as the community finishes building out, builder contracts differ from resale purchase agreements. Our new construction buyer's agent guide explains what a buyer's agent should be reviewing on your behalf even when the builder has its own sales team on site.
