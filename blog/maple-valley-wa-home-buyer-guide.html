Buying a Home in Maple Valley, WA
A 2026 Buyer's Guide

Shi Hao Liu
Licensed WA Real Estate Broker ・ Kelly Right Real Estate ・ Updated November 11, 2026

Maple Valley is a city of about 28,000 residents in southeast King County, built around Lake Wilderness and the Cedar River foothills roughly 25 miles from downtown Seattle. Zillow put the typical home value here at $763,935 as of September 30, 2026 — well below the King County-wide figure of $835,385 — which makes it one of the more affordable options left within commuting range of Seattle and the Eastside, especially for buyers chasing the top-rated Tahoma School District. Here's what to know before you buy.

Where Maple Valley fits on the map

Maple Valley was incorporated on August 31, 1997, in southeast King County, roughly 25 miles southeast of downtown Seattle and about 20 miles from Bellevue via SR-169 (the Maple Valley Highway) and SR-18, both of which connect to I-405 and I-5. Because of King County's urban growth boundary lines, the city's compact 6.2-square-mile footprint is split into separate built-up sectors strung along the highway rather than one contiguous downtown core — a layout that surprises buyers expecting a traditional city center. Growth here has been fast: the population grew nearly 63% between 2000 and 2010, then climbed from 22,684 at the 2010 census to 28,013 at the 2020 census. Compared to a closer-in South King County city like Renton, Maple Valley trades a few extra highway miles for more space and a quieter, more rural feel.

Schools: the draw for families

Maple Valley is served by the Tahoma School District, which third-party ranking site SchoolDigger consistently places among the top tier of Washington's school districts, with a full five-star rating. Tahoma Senior High School and Summit Trail Middle School have both landed near the top of their respective statewide rankings in recent years. As with any district, individual school ratings shift from year to year and boundaries can change by street, so confirm the exact elementary, middle, and high school assignment for a specific address directly with the district before writing an offer.

Lake Wilderness and the valley's working-town roots

The city's namesake lake has a history rooted in the region's logging industry: in the 1890s, one of the largest logging mills in King County operated at Lake Wilderness before the site was later redeveloped as a lakeside resort. King County purchased the property in 1964, and it's now Lake Wilderness Park, home to the Lake Wilderness Arboretum, a public swimming beach, trails, and a lodge used for community events. The city also includes Lake Lucerne, Rock Creek, and part of Pipe Lake, giving it more lake and creek frontage than most of its Eastside neighbors — a draw for buyers who want water access without Eastside water-adjacent pricing.

What homes cost in Maple Valley in 2026

Zillow's home value index put the typical Maple Valley home at $763,935 as of September 30, 2026, down about 2.4% from a year earlier and roughly flat month-over-month — compared to a King County-wide typical value of $835,385 as of August 2026. That roughly $70,000 gap is a big part of why buyers priced out of the Eastside, or even out of closer-in South King County cities, increasingly look here. As with any citywide figure, it blends very different housing stock — older homes closer to the highway corridor and newer construction in the surrounding hills — so ask your agent for comparables specific to the neighborhood and home type you're considering rather than relying on a single citywide number.

The commute reality

Maple Valley's location is the main trade-off against its price and its schools. SR-169 and SR-18 connect the city to I-405 and I-5, putting downtown Seattle roughly 25 miles and Bellevue roughly 20 miles away, with off-peak drive times generally landing in the 30-40 minute range. Peak-hour traffic on SR-169 and SR-18 can add substantially more time, and transit service in and out of Maple Valley is limited and largely commuter-oriented rather than all-day. If you're weighing a longer commute against more space and a lower price, our Eastside relocation guide walks through how to think about that trade-off.

What to watch for as a buyer

  • Cedar River flood risk in low-lying areas. The Cedar River runs through the valley, and King County's flood monitoring identifies low-lying parts of the city — including the Dorre Don area along Jones Road — as prone to flooding when the river reaches higher flood stages. Check the FEMA flood zone designation for any specific address and ask about flood insurance costs before writing an offer near the river — see our flood zone and flood insurance guide for how that process works.
  • Septic and well systems in outlying areas. Unincorporated communities just outside city limits, such as Hobart, Ravensdale, and Cedar, commonly still rely on septic systems and private wells rather than municipal sewer and water. If you're looking at a property outside the core city limits, confirm which utilities actually serve it — our septic and well inspection guide covers what to check.
  • HOA dues in newer developments. Newer plats built into the surrounding hills often carry HOA dues and CC&Rs tied to shared amenities. Review the resale certificate carefully during your review period — our HOA and condo documents guide covers what to look for.
  • Inspection items on older homes. Homes closer to the original highway corridor can be considerably older, which raises the usual questions about roofing, plumbing, electrical, and foundation condition. Our common PNW inspection findings guide covers what typically turns up.
