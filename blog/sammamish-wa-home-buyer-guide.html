Buying a Home in Sammamish, WA
A 2026 Buyer's Guide

Shi Hao Liu
Licensed WA Real Estate Broker ・ Kelly Right Real Estate ・ Updated September 19, 2026

Sammamish is a family-oriented Eastside city of roughly 67,000 residents east of Redmond and Issaquah, known for top-rated schools, newer construction, and lake and forest surroundings. It's also one of the priciest cities in King County: Sammamish's median sale price ran roughly $1.5-1.6 million through 2026, nearly double the countywide median. Which school district you land in, which HOA you join, and how far you'll commute all depend heavily on exactly where in the city a home sits — so this guide walks through what actually varies block to block.

Where Sammamish fits on the Eastside

Incorporated in 1999, Sammamish sits on the plateau east of Lake Sammamish, bordered by Redmond and Issaquah. It has no freeway running through it — SR-202 and East Lake Sammamish Parkway are the main arteries connecting it to I-90 and SR-520 — which is part of why it feels quieter and more suburban than Redmond or Kirkland. That same lack of a freeway or light rail line is the trade-off buyers make for larger lots, newer homes, and some of the best-regarded public schools in the state.

Schools: which district is your house actually in?

This is the single most important thing to confirm before you fall in love with a specific Sammamish listing, because the city is split between three separate school districts and the boundaries don't track neatly with any street map most buyers would guess at:

  • Lake Washington School District generally serves the northern part of Sammamish (the same district covering Redmond and Kirkland).
  • Issaquah School District generally serves the southern part of the city.
  • Snoqualmie Valley School District covers a smaller pocket in the northeast — an area that has, in fact, spent years petitioning to be redrawn into Lake Washington School District, most recently through an ongoing appeal in King County Superior Court after the district's board again declined to move the boundary in late 2025.

Don't assume: verify the assigned elementary, middle, and high school for the specific address, not just "Sammamish," before you write an offer. Your agent, the district's own boundary lookup tool, or the listing's public records can confirm it in minutes.

Sammamish's neighborhoods

Sammamish reads less like a single downtown and more like a patchwork of planned communities, many built around a lake, golf course, or HOA-run park system:

  • Klahanie — an established, HOA-run community in southwest Sammamish (annexed into the city in 2016) with its own pools, parks, and a self-contained neighborhood feel.
  • Beaver Lake — larger, more wooded lots anchored around Beaver Lake Park, generally quieter and less densely built.
  • Trossachs — a newer planned community in eastern Sammamish with HOA-maintained streets and easy access to Soaring Eagle Regional Park's trail system.
  • Sahalee — an upscale community built around the private Sahalee Country Club and golf course.
  • The Sammamish Plateau / Town Center area — closer to the city's commercial core along 228th Ave, with a mix of home ages and easier access to shopping.

What homes cost in Sammamish in 2026

Sammamish is consistently one of the more expensive cities on the Eastside. In spring 2026, Redfin reported a median sale price around $1.6 million, and Zillow's broader home-value estimate for the same stretch of the year ran in a similar $1.57 million to $1.64 million range — compared with King County's overall median of about $840,000 in February 2026. Like much of the region, Sammamish prices softened modestly from a year earlier (roughly 3-5%, depending on the source), as higher rates cooled buyer demand somewhat. Even so, the premium over the county median reflects the larger lots, newer construction, and school reputation buyers are paying for — a very different price tier than a Redmond condo or a starter home closer to Seattle.

Why so many Sammamish listings come with an HOA

Because much of the city was built out as planned communities rather than infill development, a large share of Sammamish homes belong to a homeowners association — Klahanie and Trossachs among them. That means dues, a resale certificate, and rules on things like rentals or exterior changes are a normal part of buying here, not the exception. What to watch for as a buyer

  • Competitive offers on well-priced listings. Like much of the Eastside, a well-priced Sammamish home in a sought-after school boundary can still draw multiple offers. See how to win a bidding war on the Eastside for escalation clauses and contingency strategy.
  • Inspections on larger, older lots. Wooded lots and homes from the 1990s-2000s building boom can hide drainage, roof, or aging-sewer issues that a walkthrough won't catch. Our home inspection contingency guide covers when a sewer scope or structural inspection is worth the extra few hundred dollars.
  • Commute reality, not just distance on a map. Drive times run roughly 20-35 minutes to Redmond or Bellevue via SR-202 or I-90, and 35-55 minutes to Seattle via I-90 during peak hours — with limited bus service and no rail connection, unlike parts of Redmond, Bellevue, or Kirkland. If you're relocating for a tech job, our Eastside relocation guide covers how to weigh commute against space and schools.

Retail, parks, and everyday life

Most day-to-day shopping and dining in Sammamish is spread along 228th Ave SE/NE, anchored by the Sammamish Highlands retail center — grocery stores, coffee shops, and casual restaurants rather than a walkable core. The city adopted a Town Center Plan back in 2008 aimed at eventually giving Sammamish the walkable civic hub it's never had, and that project is still working its way through development. For outdoor life, residents lean on city and county parks and the Soaring Eagle Regional Park trail system, plus nearby Lake Sammamish, rather than a single downtown gathering spot. If a walkable town center matters more to your day-to-day than lot size, that's worth weighing against Sammamish's larger-lot, more residential character before you commit to a neighborhood.
